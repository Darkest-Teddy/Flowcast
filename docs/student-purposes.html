Flowcast · product research

Twelve reasons a BU student would open Flowcast twice

Flowcast already simulates the campus beautifully. What it does not yet do is give an individual student a recurring reason to come back. This memo works through where that reason could come from — ranked by how much of the answer is already sitting in the repository.

The finding that reorders everything else

The README promises “a student dashboard that answers where should I go?” with “today's schedule, crowd forecasts for your places, nearby events.” The public half of that is real and working. The personal half is not wired up: in web/src/data/api.ts, getSchedule, getSuggestions and getWeekStats all return an empty array, and every calendar operation — connect, sync, select, disconnect — resolves to unsupported.

So a student who signs in today gets public forecasts, the event list, clubs and Ask. Nothing that knows who they are. Every genuinely sticky purpose below needs one thing first: knowing which courses this student is taking.

The cheap unlock is course codes, not calendar OAuth

Google Calendar sync is the expensive path, and it is also the unreliable one — students keep their timetable in the registrar's system, not in a tidy calendar. But BU's published schedule is already committed to this repo, keyed by course code, with building, room, weekday and start/end times for every meeting. Let a student pick their courses from the 2,781 published codes and Flowcast can derive their entire week from published data, with no OAuth, no scraping of private accounts, and full provenance for every row.

This is one feature. It converts four of the twelve purposes below from impossible to mostly-built, and it fits the project's data-honesty rules better than calendar sync does.

Tier one

Purposes the course-code unlock would open

Each of these is largely built except for the one missing input. They are the highest return per unit of work in the whole list.

01

Will this schedule actually work?

Data ready

“I'm registering for next term. Can I physically get from my 9:05 to my 10:10?”

BU's standard passing time is fifteen minutes — visible in the data itself, where the common slots run 09:55→10:10, 10:45→11:00 and 12:05→12:20. But the Charles River campus is a mile and a half of Comm Ave. Of the 1,081 pairs of teaching buildings on that one campus, 221 are more than a fifteen-minute walk apart. A student who registers for back-to-back classes in two of them has bought a term of being late, and absolutely nothing in the registration flow warns them.

Flowcast is the only system at BU that holds all three ingredients: which room each section meets in, when, and how long the walk between any two buildings takes along real pedestrian routes. Feed it a candidate course list and it can flag time conflicts, impossible walks, and — using the existing fatigue model — which of two otherwise equivalent schedules will grind the student down.

Exists
Class meetings with rooms and times; bundled OSM walking routes; fatigue.ts (sleep pressure, circadian, activity load, rush penalty)
Missing
A course picker; walk distances for all 101 places rather than 14
Why it sticks
Not daily — but it is the highest-stakes ten minutes of a student's term, and it makes them bring friends
02

Leave now

Partly built

“Tell me when to stand up, accounting for the crowd I'll walk into.”

The notification preferences already define a packedRoute toggle. Nothing sends it. A leave-now nudge is the single most habit-forming thing a campus app can do, because it arrives without being asked for and it is right often enough to be trusted. Flowcast can do the version Google Maps cannot: adjust the departure time for a crowd that has not happened yet, because it simulated it.

Exists
packedRoute preference; walking times; hourly crowd projections; insights.ts next-class logic
Missing
The student's schedule; a delivery channel (web push)
Honesty note
Phrase as a projection — “Comm Ave is usually busy at 12:15” — never as a live measurement
03

You have 95 minutes. Here's where to spend them.

Data ready

“Three hours between classes and nowhere to be. Again.”

This is the defining commuter-student problem, and it is reported the same way everywhere: students describe doomscrolling through multi-hour gaps because finding somewhere to sit, eat or work between classes is genuinely hard. It is a ranking problem, and Flowcast has every term in it — the gap's length and endpoints, what is open then, how full each option will be, and how far it is from where the student already stands.

The repo even has the non-obvious options: outdoor spots along the Charles, BU Beach and the Esplanade lawns, already modelled as destinations with walking connectors, and already weather-aware.

Exists
Opening hours for 27 locations; hourly projections; outdoor.ts river spots; weather and temperature bands in conditions.ts
Missing
The student's schedule, so the gap can be detected at all
Why it sticks
Recurs several times a week for the exact population least served by campus today
04

The shape of your week

Partly built

“Why am I wrecked every Tuesday?”

getWeekStats returns an empty array, and the WeekStat type — value, detail, sparkline series, highlighted index — is already designed for exactly this. The fatigue model is written and documented. Put them together and a student gets a legible answer to a question they feel but cannot articulate: Tuesday is bad because of three rushed transfers and a 12:20 lunch attempt at the fullest hour of the week.

Exists
WeekStat contract; fatigue.ts; the .kiro/specs/student-fatigue spec
Missing
The student's schedule; a stats endpoint behind getWeekStats
Caution
Keep it descriptive. A fatigue number presented as a health metric is a claim this data can't support

Tier two

Purposes that need no personal data at all

These work for a student who never signs in, which also makes them the ones that can grow by word of mouth. Everything they need is already committed to the repository.

05

Twenty thousand seats nobody can find

Data ready

“Everywhere is packed and I need a desk for ninety minutes.”

This is the strongest single idea in the memo. BU's study-space shortage is not a perception problem — Mugar is the campus's one main library, and student press has been writing about midterm-season crush there for years. Meanwhile the committed catalogue describes 557 distinct classrooms, 432 of them with a published seat count, adding up to 20,712 seats. During weekday daytime hours, 92% of those room-hours have no class scheduled in them.

Flowcast already knows which room is empty at 2pm on a Thursday, because it knows every class that is not in it. No other system a student can reach puts those two halves together. An “empty rooms near me, right now” view is a few hours of work on data that is already in the repo — and it addresses the pain students complain about most.

Exists
Every meeting's room, weekday and times; 432 published room capacities; building opening hours; map positions for all 101 places
Missing
An inversion of the meetings index, and one policy conversation
Honesty note
Essential: “no class scheduled” is not “the room is unlocked and you may use it.” Label it as absence of a published booking, with the unlocked state marked unknown. Check BU's room-use policy before shipping, or the feature pits students against the Registrar
06

The quiet way there

Partly built

“Route me around the crowd, not just around the distance.”

The README names this population in its opening argument — students with anxiety, sensory sensitivities or mobility needs, for whom knowing where it is quiet decides whether they leave the house. The research literature on autistic students on campus lands in the same place, and recommends precisely this accommodation: designated alternative routes that avoid crowd pinch points.

Flowcast can build them. publicCrowd.pedestrianCounts already counts walkers crossing an arbitrary screen along a route, and that machinery is validated against the City of Boston's own sidewalk counts — Comm Ave at Silber Way comes out at 85% of the observed figure. Scoring a route by projected sidewalk density instead of metres is a reweighting of a solved problem, and it is a thing no mapping app can do, because it requires having simulated the crowd in advance.

Exists
pedestrianCounts screen-crossing counts, validated against city counts; corridor geometry in campus-walk-corridors.json
Missing
Per-corridor projections exposed to the dashboard; a crowd-weighted route scorer
Why it matters
It is the clearest case where the simulation earns its keep over live sensors, and it serves the users the project says it is for
07

The best time to go

Data ready

“When is Mugar actually bearable?”

Worth learning from the one product in this space with real student adoption: Waitz, the Occuspace app deployed at UCSB, Vanderbilt, Baylor and others. Students there use three things — current level, a one-hour-ahead prediction, and historical peaks so they can plan around a favourite spot. A UCSB student called it “kind of the best thing to happen to the library in a long time.”

Flowcast's Forecasts page shows the hourly curve already. What it doesn't do is answer the question in the student's words. “Go at 3pm, not 1pm” is the same data with the conclusion drawn — and because Flowcast projects rather than senses, it can answer for next Tuesday, which no sensor-based product can.

Exists
Hourly projections per place; peak and quietest-hour computation already in Forecasts.tsx
Missing
A recommendation phrasing, and comparison across the week rather than within one day
Honesty note
Flowcast's advantage is the future; its disadvantage is the present. Don't dress a projection up as live occupancy
08

Finals mode

Partly built

“It's reading period and every chair on campus is taken.”

The academic calendar in the repo has the exact dates: study period 11–13 December 2026, finals 14–18 December, Thanksgiving recess 25–29 November. And conditions.ts already defines reading-period, finals, break and move-in as first-class academic periods the behaviour engine responds to.

Two weeks a term, demand for exactly one resource — a quiet seat — goes vertical, and student attention goes with it. A seasonal mode that combines the empty-room finder with reading-period crowd projections is the moment Flowcast is most useful and most likely to spread. It also pairs naturally with roadmap item three.

Exists
Dated academic calendar with suspended days and substitutions; academic-period types in conditions.ts
Missing
Behaviour rates that actually differ during finals, and a student-facing surface for the period
09

What the weather does to campus

Partly built

“It's pouring. Where did everyone go?”

conditions.ts models weather families and temperature bands and feeds them into behaviour — rain pushes people indoors, heat pushes them to the river. Students experience this as a hard, specific question on bad-weather days, and Boston supplies plenty of them. It is a small surfacing job on a model that already exists.

Exists
Weather kinds and temperature bands wired into the behaviour engine
Missing
A live or forecast weather feed, and a student-facing explanation of the shift

Tier three

Purposes that serve students in other roles

Smaller audiences, slower to pay off, but each reaches people who are students too — and the last one is the only purpose here that survives the semester ending.

10

Meet in the middle

Partly built

“Four of us, coming from four buildings, need somewhere to work at eight.”

Group project logistics are a real recurring cost, and the problem is well-posed: minimise total walking from everyone's last known location, subject to the place being open and projected to have room. Flowcast holds all three constraints. The hard part is social — getting four students into the same session — which is why this ranks below the solo purposes despite being conceptually tidy.

Exists
Walk distances; opening hours; capacity projections
Missing
Walk data beyond 14 places; a lightweight share mechanism that doesn't need four accounts
11

Pick a room and a time that won't collide

Data ready

“I run a club. When should we hold this so people actually come?”

The README lists event organisers as beneficiaries, and club officers are students — there is already a clubOfficer role, a club page and a club calendar. What's missing is the thing an officer actually wants: a time and venue where the audience is free, the room is big enough, and no other event is competing. The event impact machinery answers this already, just pointed the other way round.

Exists
clubOfficer role and club pages; 88 collected events, 55 at mapped venues; the full event-impact simulation
Missing
An officer-facing “suggest a slot” view over existing outputs
12

A campus dataset students can do coursework on

Data ready

“I need a real dataset with real provenance for my data-science project.”

Flowcast has accidentally built something BU's own Computing & Data Sciences students need and rarely get: a local, documented, checksummed, deterministic dataset about a place they live in, with every value labelled as published evidence or stated assumption, and a manifest recording where each one came from. That is a better teaching artifact than most of what gets assigned.

This is the only purpose in the memo that doesn't decay — it gives the project a reason to exist after the hackathon, and it turns students from users into contributors who improve the calibration the roadmap already wants improved.

Exists
Checksummed snapshots with a provenance manifest; a deterministic simulation; 522 passing tests; documented assumptions
Missing
A documented download or read-only API, and a licence statement for downstream use

Three things that would undermine the project if done carelessly

  • “Empty” must not imply “available.” The catalogue proves no class is booked. It says nothing about whether the door is unlocked or the room is permitted for study. The project's own honesty rules demand that distinction be visible, not buried.
  • Projections are not measurements. Every purpose above gets its power from forecasting the future, which is exactly where the model is least checkable. The existing published-versus-modelled labelling needs to survive into every new student-facing surface, including push notifications, where there is no room for a caveat.
  • A course list is sensitive. Course enrolment identifies a student more precisely than most data the app currently touches. The existing design — personal schedule questions answered on-device and never sent to the Ask server — is the right instinct and should constrain how the course picker stores anything.

If only one thing gets built

Build the empty-room finder. It needs no personal data, no new collection, and no account: every input is already committed. It answers the complaint BU students make most loudly, it works for someone who opens the link once from a friend, and it demonstrates the project's whole thesis — that public data, pulled together honestly, tells you something nobody could see before.

Then build the course picker, because four more purposes are waiting behind it.
